## Books

Ruby's books are older than Python's on average and it shows: the classics were written for 1.8 and 1.9, and a reader has to carry the changes since. Each entry says which chapters this library leans on and what to read with care.

**Ruby, the language**

- *Programming Ruby 3.3: The Pragmatic Programmers' Guide* — Noel Rappin, with Dave Thomas (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2024; [publisher's page ↗](https://pragprog.com/titles/ruby5/programming-ruby-3-3-5th-edition/)). The current "Pickaxe": the chapters on blocks and iterators, on classes and modules, on exceptions, on pattern matching and on Ractors are the book-length version of chapters 03, 06, 08, 09 and 12 here. The earlier editions — *Programming Ruby 1.9 & 2.0* by Dave Thomas with Chad Fowler and Andy Hunt (2013) and *Programming Ruby 1.9* (2009) — are the same book for an older Ruby.
- *The Well-Grounded Rubyist*, third edition — David A. Black and Joseph Leo III (Manning, 2019; [publisher's page ↗](https://www.manning.com/books/the-well-grounded-rubyist-third-edition)). The best single explanation of the object model in print: its chapters on the `self` and scope, on modules and program organization, on the default object and on singleton classes are what chapters 06 and 07 here measure. The first edition (2009) covers Ruby 1.9 and is still right about the model.
- *The Ruby Programming Language* — David Flanagan and Yukihiro Matsumoto (O'Reilly, 2008). Co-written by Ruby's author as 1.9 arrived: the chapters on expressions and operators, on statements and control structures, on methods, procs, lambdas and closures, and on classes and modules are still the most precise description of the semantics that chapters 02, 03 and 09 here test. *Read with care:* Ruby 1.9 — no keyword arguments, no refinements, no pattern matching, `Fixnum` and `Bignum` still separate.
- *Eloquent Ruby* — Russ Olsen (Addison-Wesley, 2011). The idioms: the chapters on `?` and `!` methods, on `respond_to?` and duck typing, on `method_missing`, on `instance_eval` and on building DSLs are the reading behind chapters 10 and 16. *Read with care:* Ruby 1.9 era, and its advice on monkey patching predates refinements.
- *Metaprogramming Ruby 2* — Paolo Perrotta (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2014; [publisher's page ↗](https://pragprog.com/titles/ppmetr2/metaprogramming-ruby-2/)). The book of chapter 10: "The Object Model", "Methods" (`define_method`, `method_missing`, ghost methods), "Blocks" (`instance_eval`, bindings), "Class Definitions" (singleton classes, class macros, hooks). Its "spells" appendix names every technique this library measures.
- *Polished Ruby Programming* — Jeremy Evans (Packt, 2021). By the maintainer of Sequel and Roda: the chapters on choosing a data structure, on designing method APIs, on `Comparable`/`Enumerable`, on exceptions and on metaprogramming trade-offs, written for Ruby 3.
- *Practical Object-Oriented Design: An Agile Primer Using Ruby*, second edition — Sandi Metz (Addison-Wesley, 2018; [author's page ↗](https://www.poodr.com/)). Design rather than syntax: the chapters on duck typing, on inheritance versus composition and on modules are where chapter 06's mechanisms turn into decisions.
- *Confident Ruby* and *Exceptional Ruby* — Avdi Grimm (self-published, 2013 and 2011). The first is about method structure — collecting input, performing work, delivering results — and is the source of the `Array()`/`Integer()` conversion-function pattern in [Duck typing](../01_Objects_and_Values/duck_typing_and_respond_to/README.md); the second is the book of chapter 08: the failure lifecycle, `raise` and `rescue` in detail, `ensure`, `retry`, exception hierarchies.
- *Ruby Under a Microscope* — Pat Shaughnessy (No Starch Press, 2013; [author's page ↗](https://patshaughnessy.net/ruby-under-a-microscope)). The book of chapter 11: how the parser and YARV compiler work, what a `Binding` and a closure are in memory, how `method_missing` and the method cache work, and how the garbage collector runs. *Read with care:* written for Ruby 2.0 — before Prism, YJIT, Ractors and the compacting GC — but the diagrams of the object model are unchanged.
- *Refactoring: Ruby Edition* — Jay Fields, Shane Harvie, Martin Fowler and Kent Beck (Addison-Wesley, 2009); *Ruby Best Practices* — Gregory Brown (O'Reilly, 2009); *Ruby Cookbook*, second edition — Lucas Carlson and Leonard Richardson (O'Reilly, 2015). Three books of worked recipes, useful once the mechanisms are clear.

**Ruby, older and introductory** — on the shelf, and still readable for the parts of the language that have not moved

- *The Book of Ruby* — Huw Collingbourne (No Starch Press, 2011); *Beginning Ruby: From Novice to Professional* — Peter Cooper (Apress, several editions); *Learning Ruby* — Michael Fitzgerald (O'Reilly, 2007); *Everyday Scripting with Ruby* — Brian Marick (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2007); *Practical Ruby Projects* — Topher Cyll (Apress, 2008); *Best of Ruby Quiz* — James Edward Gray II (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2006), whose puzzles are still good exercises. *Read with care:* all Ruby 1.8 — hashes were unordered, `String#each` existed, `require 'set'` was needed, symbols and strings behaved differently as hash keys in several APIs.
- *Text Processing with Ruby* — Rob Miller (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2015) — belongs to the [Ruby text library ↗](https://masiarek.github.io/ruby-text-learning-library/08_Resources/index.html) and is listed there.

**Python, for the comparison**

- *Fluent Python*, second edition — Luciano Ramalho (O'Reilly, 2022). The Python book that answers the same questions this library asks of Ruby: the chapters on the data model, on functions as first-class objects, on decorators and closures, on dynamic attributes and properties, on attribute descriptors, on class metaprogramming and on concurrency models are the *Compared with Python* sections of chapters 03, 07, 10 and 12 at book length.
- *Python Distilled* — David Beazley (Addison-Wesley, 2021). Short and exact about the language, especially the chapters on functions, on classes and objects and on modules and packages.
- *Effective Python*, third edition — Brett Slatkin (Addison-Wesley, 2024). Ninety items, each the size of a page here; the ones on closures, on default arguments, on `__init_subclass__` and on `__getattr__` versus `__getattribute__` are the Python halves of pages in chapters 02, 03 and 10.
- *CPython Internals* — Anthony Shaw (Real Python, 2021). The Python twin of *Ruby Under a Microscope*: the compiler, the evaluation loop, the GIL, memory management.
